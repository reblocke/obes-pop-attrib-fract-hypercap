BMI–hypercapnia with encounter-setting adjustment

All models add ED/inpatient setting. Primary and BMI ≥30 secondary use their fixed index populations. Investigator interpretation and figure approval remain pending. No abstract text is updated here.

F0-primary-gas

F0-primary-gas. Nested gas-evaluable populations at the fixed first qualifying 2022 emergency/inpatient encounter, one patient per index. The starting box is the existing primary source-entry cohort, not all TriNetX acute-care patients. Existing eligibility and index selection remain unchanged. Analysis samples retain the specified adjustment-completeness rules; any adjustment exclusions are listed separately from unavailable gas evidence. The arterial population includes patients with both arterial and venous results. Venous-only patients remain in the combined-gas analysis; patients without qualifying pH co-observation remain in the arterial analysis. No usable gas does not establish that no test was ordered or drawn. Normal and elevated PCO₂ are eligible; the last stage requires usable arterial pH, not acidemia. Usable arterial PCO₂ and pH must share at least one recorded date within that same index encounter; the date need not be its first calendar day. Same recorded-day co-occurrence; not verified same-sample pairing. Results may originate from different samples or treatment periods. Counts and evidence statuses come from the cohort ledger; unavailable recorded-day evidence is not zero.

F1-main

F1-main. Hypercapnia across the range of BMIs in the selected primary historical source-entry population at fixed first 2022 emergency/inpatient indexes. BMI: body mass index; ABG: arterial blood gas; VBG: venous blood gas; PCO₂: partial pressure of carbon dioxide; CI: confidence interval. A: arterial PCO₂ ≥45 or venous PCO₂ ≥50 mm Hg (model/reference N = 126,074). B: arterial PCO₂ ≥45 mm Hg (N = 71,318); B includes patients with both specimens; these samples overlap and must not be summed. C: joint multinomial components in patients with usable arterial PCO₂ and arterial pH on a common recorded date (N = 53,607). Blue height gives hypercapnia with documented same-day acidemia (pH <7.35); orange thickness gives hypercapnia without such a day. Any eligible day with both findings takes priority. The upper boundary and gray 95% interval describe their total, not the orange component alone. Different samples or treatment periods may contribute. Same recorded-day co-occurrence; not verified same-sample pairing. D: separate ABG (red, solid) and VBG (blue, long-dashed) models describe usable captured PCO₂ evidence across the full adjustment-complete primary reference (N = 380,206). Obtained denotes a captured, usable PCO₂ result; tests without usable captured results are not identified. Either or both specimens may be available. Normal and elevated results count, with no pH requirement, at any time within the fixed encounter. All models reuse the primary fixed three-degree-of-freedom BMI spline and adjust for age, sex, race/ethnicity, geographic region and encounter setting (EMER reference; IMP inpatient). A–C standardize over their own outcome-evaluable reference; D over all adjustment-complete primary patients. Shading and table intervals are pointwise model-based 95% confidence intervals conditional on the empirical reference and fixed basis, not intervals for a BMI 30–40 contrast. Frequency strips describe each panel’s corresponding reference. The vertical line marks BMI 30 (kg/m²); dotted/faded segments indicate sparse or out-of-range support. Full saved curve ranges are retained. Historical selection, selective measurement, treatment timing and residual confounding limit interpretation; these results do not establish acute respiratory failure, compensation, causal effects or physiological outcomes in untested patients.
